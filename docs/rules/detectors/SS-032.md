# SS-032 - Redundant require_auth in loop

- **Severity:** low
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Category:** authorization

## Problem

A contract entry point calls `require_auth` (or `require_auth_for_args`) inside a
loop body. Every iteration repeats an authorization check even though the
operation and its authorized actor do not change. This wastes budget on a
resource-constrained network, and the repeated per-item check often signals that
authorization should be hoisted and bound to the operation's arguments once.

Repeated authorization is not automatically a vulnerability, but it is rarely
intentional and makes the authorization policy harder to reason about.

## How it is detected

The detector visits public functions in `#[contractimpl]` blocks. It tracks the
lexical nesting depth of `for`, `while`, and `loop` expressions and reports a
method call named `require_auth` or `require_auth_for_args` when the depth is
greater than zero. Authentication calls before or after a loop, and calls in
private helpers, are not reported.

## Limitations

- This is a syntactic, lexical check. It does not prove that the authorized actor
  or arguments are loop-invariant; it only proves the call is textually inside a
  loop.
- Closures passed to iterator adapters such as `for_each` are not `for`/`while`/
  `loop` expressions and are not reported.
- Macro-generated loops and authorization calls are not inspected.
- It cannot tell whether the contract authorizes a different actor per iteration
  on purpose.

## False positives

- A loop that genuinely authorizes a distinct actor for each item, for example a
  batch approval flow. Review the finding and suppress it with a justified inline
  directive if the per-item check is required.
- A loop whose body runs at most once in practice but is expressed as a loop.

## Remediation

Authorize once before the loop, or bind the complete argument set the operation
depends on:

```rust
admin.require_auth();

for recipient in recipients.iter() {
    transfer(&env, &recipient, &amount);
}
```

If each iteration really does require a different actor, document why and use an
inline suppression so the decision is visible in review.
